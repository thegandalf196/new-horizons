## Core distinction

**Miniature fantasy still-life paintings, not pictograms.**

The target is late-1990s Western fantasy illustration: richly modeled physical
objects, painterly ornament, theatrical illumination and dense emblematic
composition reduced to a small raster image. Do not equate low output resolution
with SNES-style pixel construction, hard polygon edges or flat MS Paint fills.
Do not equate polished modeling with modern jeweled mobile-game buttons either.

For secondary skills, the object itself carries the idea: substantial books,
folded cloth, feathers, parchment, metal, candles or other meaningful Sorcery
objects. A clever outline or abstract geometric symbol is not enough. Bookmark
embroidery may simplify that approved motif; the selected-school header should
be a composed illustration, not the skill icon enlarged onto empty space.

## Visual criteria to test at master and native sizes

- Strong volume without photographic realism: lit and shadowed planes, believable
  reflections, material thickness and occlusion.
- Dramatic chiaroscuro: one dominant illuminated side, deep coherent shadows,
  small brilliant highlights. Not universal glow, uniform gradients or neon rims.
- Saturated local colors without neon: royal blue, blood red, emerald, gold,
  ivory, steel gray as appropriate to the chosen composition, not all at once.
- Warm dark contextual surfaces: parchment, leather, cloth, earthy brown or
  subdued colored surroundings; avoid objects floating in empty flat UI fields.
- One dominant central semantic object or coherent small group fills most of the
  icon. Richness comes from modeling/materials, not excessive tiny objects.
- Mild heroic exaggeration: substantial books and emphatic readable objects.
- Distinct material behavior: aged metal is not glass, cloth is not parchment.
- Soft painterly boundaries defined by value/light, not uniform vector outlines.
- Older fantasy-book/RPG illustration character rather than contemporary minimal
  symbols, glossy game-store icons or stylized blocky concept sketches.
